# Kallaway Hooks for Founder-Led Video

Create hooks that earn attention from the intended buyer and lead honestly into the recorded body. Optimize for qualified attention and business relevance, not empty reach.

The Kallaway mechanics in this skill are third-party heuristics. They are not proven for the current account until account-level retention and business-outcome evidence supports them. Read [references/provenance.md](references/provenance.md) when evaluating source claims, attribution, or validation status.

Read [references/kallaway-frameworks.md](references/kallaway-frameworks.md) before generating or critiquing hooks.

## Required inputs

Use the supplied topic, transcript, draft, or recording brief as the source of truth. Infer the audience from the material when it is clear. Ask one question only when the intended audience or actual payoff cannot be determined safely.

When a body or transcript exists, identify before writing:

- The single subject
- The intended viewer
- The recognizable problem, situation, or desire
- The strongest client-owned point of view
- The story, example, or proof actually present
- The payoff the body delivers
- The desired next step

Do not invent evidence, outcomes, urgency, controversy, or a stronger conclusion than the body supports.

## Founder-content gates

For service-business founder content:

1. Make the owner recognize a visible task, handoff, interruption, consequence, or desired outcome within the first five seconds.
2. Lead with the owner's lived situation. Introduce AI, automation, systems, or other mechanisms after recognition unless the owner naturally used that language first.
3. Preserve the speaker's actual perspective and experience. Research and creator frameworks may shape delivery, but they cannot manufacture a point of view.
4. Keep story hooks when the story is the proof. Do not mechanically replace every `I` with `you`.
5. Make the opening sound spoken. Avoid polished LinkedIn prose, generic motivation, hype, and borrowed creator voice.
6. Never use em dashes or en dashes in reader-facing copy.
7. Distinguish attention evidence from lead, sales, or revenue evidence.
8. The hook must connect directly to the next spoken line and the eventual payoff. Reject bait-and-switch options.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose the current hook, if supplied, against clarity, relevance, contrast, specificity, spoken delivery, and continuity.
2. Define the one question the opening should plant in the viewer's mind.
3. Choose an executable first-frame visual before finalizing the words.
4. Generate three materially different options by default:
   - Owner-recognition or desire
   - Contrarian reframe
   - Story or proof
5. Use different Kallaway archetypes when the source supports them. Do not force an archetype that requires missing proof or an unavailable visual.
6. Recommend one option based on audience fit, body continuity, and filming practicality.
7. Label every option `hypothesis`, `validated`, or `proven`:
   - `hypothesis`: structurally plausible with no relevant performance evidence
   - `validated`: supported by a dated observation or above-baseline account result
   - `proven`: repeated, attributable retention evidence for a similar audience, platform, and format

Views alone cannot raise the evidence grade.

## Default output

For each option, provide only what helps the speaker record or edit it:

- Hook type and evidence grade
- Exact spoken opening
- Short on-screen text
- First-frame visual direction
- The single planted question
- One sentence explaining how it hands off to the body

Then name the recommended option and the concrete reason it best serves the intended buyer. Keep analysis compressed unless the user requests a deeper breakdown.

If the user asks only for raw hook material, do not turn it into polished final copy. If the user asks for final hooks, provide recordable wording.

## Boundaries

- Do not rewrite the full script unless requested.
- Do not create, edit, render, schedule, publish, or upload supporting assets without explicit authorization.
- Do not write results into the hook library, content history, ClickUp, or another external system without explicit authorization.
- Do not claim a framework is proven because it came from Kallaway or another successful creator.
