# Adapted Kallaway Hook Mechanics

Use these as selectable mechanics, not mandatory formulas. The body, buyer, proof, and available visual decide which mechanism fits.

## Core test

A useful hook creates both:

1. Immediate topic clarity
2. Targeted curiosity for the intended viewer

The viewer should understand the subject and form one specific question that the next section begins answering.

## Three-beat contrast structure

Use when the body contains a genuine reframe:

1. Context: Establish the topic or recognizable situation.
2. Pivot: Introduce tension with a short contrast.
3. Reframe: Reveal the surprising direction without spending the full payoff.

The pivot is setup, not decorative suspense. Remove it when two beats are stronger than three.

## Four-layer alignment

Design the opening as one coordinated unit:

- First-frame visual
- Spoken words
- On-screen text
- Sound or intentional silence

All layers should communicate the same subject and tension. A clever spoken line cannot rescue an unrelated visual.

## Six archetypes

| Archetype | Useful contrast | Use only when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fortune teller | Present versus plausible future | The body supports a bounded prediction |
| Experimenter | Old approach versus observed result | A real test or experience exists |
| Teacher | Familiar problem versus practical correction | The speaker can deliver a specific method |
| Magician | Expected visual versus pattern interrupt | The visual is executable and relevant |
| Investigator | Common understanding versus sourced finding | The research or discovery can be attributed |
| Contrarian | Common belief versus client-owned view | The speaker genuinely disagrees and can explain why |

Archetypes can be combined, but one should remain dominant so the opening does not split attention.

## Desire and owner-recognition hooks

A desire hook works when the viewer recognizes both a relatable person and a believable outcome. For service-business content, ground the desire in an owner-visible result such as stronger recruiting, better-fit opportunities, trusted local authority, faster lead response, smoother handoffs, or reclaimed time.

Avoid naked promises about money, freedom, or transformation. Use the closest honest proxy the body can demonstrate.

## Four failure modes

- Delay: The actual subject arrives too late.
- Confusion: The viewer has to decode the sentence.
- Irrelevance: The intended buyer cannot recognize why it matters.
- Disinterest: The subject is clear but contains no meaningful tension, contrast, consequence, or unanswered question.

Fix the specific failure. Do not add drama automatically.

## Single-subject, single-question check

Before recommending a hook, answer:

- What exact subject will every reasonable viewer identify?
- What one question should they ask next?
- Does the next spoken line continue that question?
- Does the body resolve it without changing subjects?

If reasonable viewers could form incompatible expectations, rewrite the hook.

## First-frame visual prompts

Prefer proof-bearing visuals already available:

- The person, place, or object in the story
- A real screenshot, result, document, or before-and-after state
- A simple physical demonstration
- A short phrase on screen that sharpens the spoken subject

Do not prescribe constant motion. The speaker's face and delivery may be the strongest visual when emotion or credibility is the point.

## Evidence and iteration

Treat every new structure as a hypothesis. Evaluate it using platform and account context, including early hold rate, average watch time, completion, rewatches, shares, qualified conversations, leads, and sales when attribution exists.

Compare against the account baseline and similar formats. Do not convert a creator's reported views or leads into proof that the same structure will work for the current account.
