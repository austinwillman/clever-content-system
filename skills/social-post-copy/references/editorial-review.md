# Editorial Review for Social Copy

Use these decision checks on the actual draft, not as a score that automatically approves it. The active client's source, voice, and current corrections control the result.

## Source pass

1. For each material claim, identify its source and speaker. Would it remain true if the surrounding passage were shown?
2. Identify every claimed action, result, feeling, and recurring behavior. Is it observed, quoted, inferred, hypothetical, or merely proposed? Preserve that distinction in wording.
3. Check the cover, opening, body, and ending as one promise. Does the post deliver the same subject without implying a result the video never shows?
4. Remove invented dialogue, quantitative proof, identities, or confidential details. A paraphrase must not appear as an exact quotation.

An interpretation may be offered as interpretation. It may not become a fabricated confession in first person. Evidence sufficiency and honesty are hard gates.

## Voice and argument pass

**Author test:** Name the specific judgment this author contributes. If the draft only reports what happened in a video, rebuild around the author's actual diagnosis.

**Substitution test:** Could another consultant publish the post unchanged? If yes, identify the source-specific observation, reasoning, or phrase that was lost. Restore substance before adding colorful language.

**Read-aloud test:** Does this resemble the author's evidenced cadence? Keep necessary transitions and ordinary sentences. Fix both polished corporate prose and stacks of artificially punchy fragments.

**Framework test:** Can the structure be explained by the idea's needs? If its only justification is a popular formula, simplify it. Framework names do not belong in the copy by default.

**Landing test:** Remove the last question or CTA temporarily. If the post becomes stronger, leave it out. A question earns its place when its answer would change a relevant decision or reveal a specific experience.

**Batch test:** Compare posts side by side. Reject repeated opening syntax, identical paragraph beats, and interchangeable endings unless the user asked for a consistent series format.

## Common failures and repairs

| Failure | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| A summary wearing a hook | State the author's judgment and retain only the scene needed to substantiate it. |
| Borrowed empathy with no diagnostic value | Replace reassurance with the specific constraint the author noticed. |
| Provocation exceeds evidence | Reduce certainty or scope while retaining the real tension. |
| A proposed tool becomes an established personal habit | Restore future or conditional language. |
| Client voice becomes a costume | Remove added slang or bravado; restore source-backed reasoning and cadence. |
| Every post asks for comments | End on the useful consequence or next move already earned by the body. |
| Missing demand data gets a loosely related keyword | Leave the mapping unresolved; never disguise an editorial match as validated demand. |

## Behavioral evaluation

For a substantial skill change, use private, authorized samples to exercise:

- A guest describes a problem and the author offers a metaphor. Preserve both owners.
- The author proposes a future AI use. Do not fabricate an established routine or observed result.
- The source is incomplete. Narrow the post or request the missing evidence rather than hallucinating the bridge.
- The user supplies an exact approved opening. Preserve it even when another hook might score higher.
- Two posts have different voices or contexts. Do not leak one client's identity or style into the other.
- No relevant keyword exists. Deliver authorized copy with honest metadata limitations.

Compare the resulting passages against source and user feedback. File structure checks alone do not validate voice. Keep real samples and resulting copy outside the public skill repository.
