# Owner Recognition Filter

Use this gate before topic scoring, hook writing, scripting, or ClickUp creation. The goal is not to make technology easier to explain. Make the owner's lived business problem impossible to miss.

## Tension map structure

Every candidate must define all ten fields from transcript evidence:

1. **Real quote:** One short verbatim owner quote that reveals the belief, pain, or recognizable situation. Preserve the speaker's words. Remove filler with an ellipsis only when meaning is unchanged. Use brackets for a confidential redaction. Never put a cleaned paraphrase in quotation marks.
2. **Objection:** The specific reason the owner thinks the current process is necessary, acceptable, too custom, or too risky to change.
3. **Confusion:** The mistaken diagnosis, hidden assumption, or category error. Keep this distinct from the objection.
4. **Frustration:** The recurring operational moment and its human or commercial cost. Prefer a job, handoff, approval, interruption, re-entry, delay, mistake, or week-off consequence.
5. **Aha:** The source-supported realization the client surfaced, not a generic lesson added later.
6. **Unique POV:** The reframe the client originated, materially developed, or clearly adopted. It must reveal how the client thinks about the business, not merely what a tool can do.
7. **Owner outcome:** The concrete change in time, control, capacity, team confidence, customer experience, revenue protection, reduced risk, or owner-optional operation.
8. **Raw hook material:** Exact phrases, recognizable moments, feared consequences, contrasts, stakes, and questions. These remain ingredients unless the client owner explicitly requests final hooks.
9. **`not_about`:** The technical, tactical, or educational framing that would weaken the piece.
10. **`really_about`:** The owner-level human or business truth the piece must land.

If the transcript does not support all ten fields, hold the idea or retrieve more context. Never manufacture missing tension.

## Five-second raise-hand test

The opening territory must make the intended owner think, "That is my shop," within five spoken seconds and before the mechanism is explained.

It must contain at least one of:

- A task the owner personally repeats
- A phrase the owner actually says
- A handoff waiting on the owner
- A recurring interruption or exception
- A feared consequence involving money, customers, crew, time, or control
- A visible moment from the workday

Do not begin with a broad audience label, a technology category, or an abstract efficiency claim.

### Failing territory

"Disconnected systems create operational inefficiency."

The owner must translate every word before deciding whether it applies.

### Passing territory

"You enter the sold job in the tracker. Then again in the commission sheet. Then again in payroll."

The owner recognizes the moment before hearing the diagnosis.

## Translate system language into owner language

In the opening, prefer the owner's nouns and verbs: lead, call, estimate, job, crew, customer, schedule, invoice, payment, payroll, handoff, approval, mistake, wait.

Delay technical terms such as AI, automation, API, integration, database, CRM, dashboard, workflow, agent, trigger, and model until after recognition. Use one early only when the source owner used it naturally and the intended audience will understand it without translation.

Translate mechanisms into visible actions:

- `integration` becomes `the sold job reaches payroll without you copying it`
- `exception handling` becomes `the normal jobs keep moving and the odd one comes back to you`
- `single source of truth` becomes `the team knows which number is the real one`
- `automated workflow` becomes `the next step happens without someone chasing it`

## Recognition score

Score each dimension from 0 to 2.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real owner voice | No source quote | Quote is generic or weak | Short quote reveals the actual belief or pain |
| Visible moment | Abstract problem | Situation is implied | Owner can picture the task or interruption |
| Objection clarity | Missing | Broad resistance | Specific reason the owner resists change |
| Confusion clarity | Missing or identical to objection | Present but vague | Precise mistaken diagnosis or assumption |
| Frustration and stakes | No consequence | General annoyance | Clear cost to time, money, team, control, or risk |
| Client reframe | Generic advice | Useful but familiar | Distinct and source-supported POV |
| Owner outcome | Feature or mechanism | General benefit | Concrete change in the owner's working life or business |

Pass at 11 out of 14 or higher. Automatically fail if real owner voice, visible moment, client reframe, or owner outcome scores 0. A high Topic Score cannot compensate for recognition failure.

## Plain-language playback

A non-technical owner should be able to answer all four questions without translation:

1. What is happening in the business?
2. Why is it frustrating or risky?
3. What did the client notice that the owner had not named yet?
4. What becomes easier, safer, or less owner-dependent afterward?

If any answer requires explaining the technology, rewrite from the owner's side of the counter.

## Preserve respect

Do not frame owner-dependency as stupidity, laziness, or failure. The owner often became the system because they cared about speed, quality, customers, or the team.

- Show why the current process made sense.
- Name where it stopped scaling.
- Preserve the judgment worth keeping.
- Move repeatable work into the system and return only true exceptions to the owner.

The final feeling should be recognition and relief, not embarrassment.
