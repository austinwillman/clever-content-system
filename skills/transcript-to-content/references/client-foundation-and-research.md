# Client Foundation and Research

Use this reference for one-time client onboarding, self-service discovery, foundation validation, and the persistent topic bank.

## Two research passes

### Foundation validation

Run once before the topic bank becomes active. Run it again only after a material strategy change, a scheduled strategic review, or topic-bank depletion.

The purpose is to validate:

- ICP identity and buying context
- Exact pain, objection, and desired-outcome language
- Positioning and offers
- Client-owned point of view
- Proof and authority
- Pillars, voice, CTA destinations, and publication boundaries

### Batch signal scan

Run before each content batch. This is an abbreviated scan for timeliness, not a replacement for foundation research.

Look for:

- New questions and repeated phrases from the ICP
- Recent comment and inbox threads
- Search-query movement
- Competitor or industry conversations
- Client content that materially outperformed its baseline
- Hook observations with retention evidence
- Events that create a timely angle for an existing topic

## Connector-first self-service intake

When the client is onboarding without a coached interview:

1. List the workspace connectors and local sources that are actually available.
2. Request read-only access only to the client-approved scope.
3. Search likely source locations before asking the user any intake question.
4. Extract a candidate answer with exact evidence references.
5. Mark each field `confirmed`, `conflicted`, `inferred`, or `unresolved`.
6. Do not treat an inference as confirmed.
7. Ask one consolidated set of questions containing only conflicted and unresolved fields.
8. Present the completed foundation for client approval.
9. Persist the approved foundation and then build the topic bank.

Search in this priority order when available:

1. Customer calls, support conversations, reviews, surveys, and CRM notes
2. Search Console queries, site-search data, social inbox threads, and audience comments
3. Proposals, sales decks, service pages, case studies, and offer documents
4. Strategy documents, brand guides, founder notes, and prior content
5. The public website and attributed public market sources

Verbatim customer language outranks polished marketing language for pain and objections. Client documents remain authoritative for approved positioning, offers, proof, and boundaries.

## Required foundation fields

- ICP and buying context
- Positioning
- Offers and offer availability
- Recurring problems and desired outcomes
- Proof inventory and claim limits
- Voice and examples
- Content pillars
- CTA classes and live destinations
- Confidentiality and prohibited topics

Every field stores a value and `evidence_refs`. Empty evidence is acceptable only when the client explicitly supplies and approves the answer.

## Conflict handling

Do not silently reconcile conflicts. Show the competing evidence, source dates, and likely reason for the mismatch. Ask the client which version is current.

Examples:

- The website names one ICP while recent proposals target another.
- A strategy document describes an offer that the sales team no longer sells.
- A case study claims an outcome that has no approved proof source.
- The founder's language differs materially from recent customer language.

## Topic-bank construction

Every topic-bank entry requires:

- Topic ID and active status
- Pillar
- ICP problem
- Verbatim or faithful buyer language
- Client-owned POV
- Owner outcome
- Evidence IDs
- Last validation date

The topic bank is the durable baseline. Batch research may add a timely angle or new evidence, but it must not rewrite the baseline without preserving source history.

## Search Target pass

Treat every public video transcript as searchable content. Before an approved topic becomes a Recording Card, match it to one real audience query. Do not turn a clever phrase into a keyword without evidence.

Research in this order when available:

1. Search Console queries and site-search data from the client's owned properties
2. Ahrefs or another keyword platform with dated volume, difficulty, and intent evidence
3. YouTube search terms, vidIQ, native platform search, and public search suggestions
4. Metricool inbox threads, audience comments, customer calls, reviews, and CRM language
5. A dated manual search-results observation when the stronger sources are unavailable

For every selected target, store:

- One primary query
- One natural spoken phrase
- Search intent
- Two or three supporting terms or entities
- Validation status: `validated`, `provisional`, `qualitative_only`, or `unavailable`
- Evidence source, URL or report, and observation date
- Search volume and difficulty only when the source actually supplies them
- Evidence limitations

Choose the query by ICP fit, pillar fit, topic fit, intent, demand evidence, platform portability, and spoken naturalness. Reject a larger keyword when it attracts the wrong audience or pulls the video outside its pillar.

The exact query does not need to be repeated. Prompt the speaker to use its natural spoken form once when it fits. The title, captions, description, transcript, and on-screen language should describe the same question without keyword stuffing.

## Evidence limits

- A transcript proves that a person said something. It does not prove market prevalence.
- A comment thread proves observed language. It does not prove demand size.
- Search volume shows query activity. It does not prove buying intent or conversion.
- A manual search result proves that phrasing exists in indexed content. It does not prove search volume, difficulty, or audience fit.
- Views show distribution. They do not prove retention, trust, or revenue.
- A client claim is not public proof until its supporting evidence is approved.
