# Live Hooks and Post-Interview Production

Use this reference when the provider wants hook pickups during the same recording session or production notes for a video editor.

## Hook evidence grades

- `hypothesis`: A useful structure with no client or industry retention proof.
- `validated`: Supported by a dated attributed observation or by performance above the account baseline, but without enough repeat evidence to call it proven.
- `proven`: Supported by source references and repeated retention evidence relevant to the platform, audience, and format.

Views alone cannot raise an evidence grade. Prefer early hold rate, average watch time, completion, replays, shares, and performance relative to the account baseline.

## Same-session hook loop

Riverside's final transcript is a post-recording source. Use live structured notes or a separately authorized live-transcription sidecar during the call.

For each topic:

1. Capture the owner problem, strongest client phrase, client POV, story or proof, and conclusion.
2. Match those facts to relevant hook-library entries.
3. Generate exactly three concise options:
   - Owner-recognition hook
   - Contrarian or curiosity reframe
   - Story or proof hook
4. Show the template ID and evidence grade for each option.
5. Reject any hook that promises information the recorded body does not deliver.
6. Let the provider select one or two options.
7. Put the selected wording into the teleprompter or read it to the client.
8. Record each pickup with a verbal or written take identifier.
9. Leave clean handles before and after the line.

After Riverside finishes processing, reconcile the hook against the complete transcript. Preserve the pickup wording, take ID, and body timestamps in the editor package.

## Production routes

Choose one route per video:

- `talking_head`: The client and idea carry the visual.
- `green_screen`: An attributed source visual materially strengthens proof, context, or curiosity.
- `hybrid`: Talking head remains primary and a green-screen section or B-roll supports one beat.

Do not select green screen merely because the format is currently popular. The background asset must have a job and usable rights.

## B-roll map

Every B-roll note states:

- Entry and exit timestamp
- Asset or shot needed
- Editorial purpose: prove, explain, orient, or reset attention
- Framing or crop guidance
- On-screen text, if any
- Rights or attribution status
- Fallback if the asset is unavailable

Avoid constant visual churn. If the client, emotion, or sentence is the point, stay on the client.

## Editor package quality gate

- The selected hook delivers into the body without a bait-and-switch.
- The editor can find every source clip and pickup by timestamp or take ID.
- Claims, names, and numbers are labeled for verification.
- Green-screen and B-roll sources are attributable and rights-safe.
- Captions preserve meaning and client voice.
- Creative direction fits the client's style.
- The CTA matches the manifest and is actually available.
- The brief says what not to overproduce.
