# Client Foundation Intake

Use this once per client. Search authorized evidence before asking the client a question. Ask only what remains unresolved or conflicted.

For each answer, record:

- Value
- Status: confirmed, conflicted, inferred, or unresolved
- Evidence references
- Confidence
- Follow-up required

## Business and direction

1. What does the business sell today?
2. Which offer is the priority during the next 90 days?
3. What does the client want the business or founder to become known for?
4. What business result should this content strengthen?
5. Which offers, destinations, or campaigns are actually live?

## ICP and buying context

1. Who is the primary buyer?
2. What business stage, role, industry, geography, and constraints define the best-fit buyer?
3. What event makes the problem urgent?
4. What has the buyer already tried?
5. What objections or risks slow the decision?
6. What exact phrases do customers use when describing the problem?
7. What does the buyer want to feel or be able to do after solving it?

## Problems and outcomes

1. Which problems are frequent, expensive, emotionally charged, or strategically important?
2. What visible symptoms does the buyer notice first?
3. What does the client believe is actually causing those symptoms?
4. What does inaction cost?
5. What human or commercial outcome matters most?

## Positioning and point of view

1. Why should this buyer choose the client?
2. What does the client believe that competitors commonly miss?
3. Which frameworks, methods, or phrases belong to the client?
4. Which topics can the client discuss from direct experience?
5. Which claims are opinions, and which have evidence?

## Proof and authority

1. Which case studies, testimonials, results, credentials, and examples are approved for public use?
2. Where is the supporting evidence stored?
3. Which client or customer identities must remain private?
4. Which numbers require verification before publication?
5. Which outcomes are planned or estimated rather than achieved?

## Voice and creative direction

1. What should the client sound like?
2. Which existing content sounds most like them?
3. Which words, tones, or creator styles should be avoided?
4. How comfortable are they with direct-to-camera, interview clips, green screen, demonstrations, and screen recordings?
5. What editing pace and visual treatment fit the brand?

## Pillars and CTA

1. Which recurring content pillars support the positioning and offer?
2. What owner problem and outcome belongs to each pillar?
3. Which CTAs are appropriate for reach, relationship, capture, conversation, and conversion?
4. Which CTA destinations exist and have been verified?
5. Which CTA promises are unavailable or prohibited?

## Boundaries and approvals

1. Which topics are confidential, regulated, legally sensitive, or off-brand?
2. Who approves factual claims, creative treatment, and final publication?
3. What does recording consent authorize?
4. What requires separate publication approval?
5. Where will source files, approvals, and final assets live?

## Research validation gaps

Ask these only after the connector and source review:

1. Which conclusions are based only on internal assumptions?
2. Which customer-language findings appear in only one source?
3. Which ICP, offer, proof, or positioning records conflict?
4. Which research sources are unavailable, stale, or export-only?
5. What must be confirmed before the foundation can be approved?
